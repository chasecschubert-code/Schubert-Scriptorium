A Witness to the Truth

My fascination with biblical manuscripts isn't simply about history—it's about the faithfulness of God.

I created Schubert Scriptorium to bridge the gap between seeing a photograph of an ancient manuscript in a textbook and experiencing the physical reality of history. A manuscript fragment is more than ink on an old piece of material. It is a tangible connection to the people, places, and faith that carried the words of Scripture across the centuries.

My hope is that these facsimiles allow you to hold a small, powerful echo of the past—and to remember the countless unknown and faithful believers who carefully copied, preserved, and passed along the Scriptures so that we might have them today.

Why P52 Matters

Generally dated to the early second century, Papyrus 52 (P52) is the oldest surviving manuscript fragment containing a portion of the New Testament. Though only a few inches in size, this tiny fragment gives us a remarkable glimpse into the early transmission of the Gospel of John.

And what it preserves is extraordinary. P52 contains portions of John 18—the account of Jesus' trial before Pontius Pilate, just hours before His crucifixion. On the verso (back) of the fragment are the words of Jesus:

“For this I was born and for this I have come into the world—to bear witness to the truth.”

Immediately afterward comes Pilate's famous question: “What is truth?”

There is something profoundly fitting about this particular passage surviving in one of the earliest fragments of the New Testament. Jesus speaks of bearing witness to the truth, and two thousand years later, this tiny piece of papyrus continues to bear witness to the words that were written down about Him.

About Me

Hey there! Thanks for visiting this odd but unique little corner of the internet.

My name is Chase Schubert, and I'm a lifelong learner and student of Scripture, history, antique maps, manuscripts, and the stories that connect us to the past. With a degree in History Education from Abilene Christian University and experience as a U.S. history teacher, I've always loved making history tangible, memorable, and meaningful.

What began as a personal fascination with biblical manuscripts—and an attempt to teach myself ancient Greek—eventually turned into a project of recreating P52 by hand. After many experiments and iterations, I began making facsimiles that capture not only the appearance of the original manuscript, but something of the experience of holding a tangible piece of the ancient world.

Every facsimile is handcrafted by me personally, carefully cut, prepared, and finished by hand. I don't make these simply because ancient manuscripts are fascinating. I make them because I believe the story behind them matters. Behind every surviving manuscript is a story of transmission, preservation, and faithfulness. Behind the ink are generations of people who read these words, taught them to their children, treasured them, and passed them on.
