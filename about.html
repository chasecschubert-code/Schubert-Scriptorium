A Witness to the Truth

My fascination with biblical manuscripts isn't just about history—it’s about the faithfulness of God. I created Schubert Scriptorium to bridge the gap between a photo in a textbook and the physical reality of history. I want people to be able to hold a powerful echo of the past and be reminded of the countless unknown yet faithful believers who carefully transcribed the Scriptures so that we might have them today.

Why P52?

Discovered in Egypt in the early 20th Century and dated (by both Christian and non-Christian scholars) to the early 2nd Century, P52 is a testimony to how quickly the Gospel of John was being copied and circulated within a single generation of the Apostles. The fragment captures a pivotal moment: Jesus’ trial before Pilate, hours before our Lord's crucifixion.

On the verso (back) of this fragment, we find the very words of Jesus declaring His mission: "...to bear witness to the truth." It is a profound providence that this specific fragment was preserved—recording the conversation that leads directly to Pilate’s famous question: "What is truth?"

About Me

Hey there! Thanks for visiting this odd but unique site of mine. My name is Chase Schubert, and I am a lifelong learner and student of Scripture, history, antique maps, manuscripts, and the stories that connect us to the past. With a degree in History Education from Abilene Christian University and former US History teacher, I’ve always loved making history tangible, memorable, and impactful. What began as a personal project exploring biblical manuscripts online and attempting to self-teach ancient Greek eventually led me to recreating P52 by hand for fun (with many iterations). Every facsimile is handcrafted by me personally, delicately and methodically. My hope is that each fascimile captivates, awes, and encourages each admirer as a testament to the reliabililty of God's Word and the faithful preservation of it, from the first Christians to us today.
