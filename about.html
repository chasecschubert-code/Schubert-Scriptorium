A Witness to the Truth

My fascination with biblical manuscripts isn't just about history—it’s about the faithfulness of God. I created Schubert Scriptorium to bridge the gap between a photo in a textbook and the physical reality of history. I want people to be able to hold a powerful and tangible echo of the past and be reminded of the generations of Christians who faithfully transcribed the Scriptures so that we might have them today.

Why P52?

Discovered in Egypt and dating to the early 2nd century, Papyrus 52 is a testimony to how quickly the Gospel of John was being copied and circulated within a single generation of the Apostles. The fragment captures a pivotal moment: Jesus’ trial before Pilate.

On the verso (back) of this fragment, we find the very words of Jesus declaring His mission: "...to bear witness to the truth." It is a profound providence that this specific fragment was preserved—recording the conversation that leads directly to Pilate’s famous question: "What is truth?"

About Me

Hey there, and thanks for visiting this odd little site of mine. I’m Chase Schubert, a lifelong learner and student of Scripture, history, antique maps, manuscripts, and the stories that connect us to the past. With a degree in History Education from Abilene Christian University and seven years as a U.S. History educator, I’ve always loved making history tangible, memorable, and meaningful. What began as a personal project studying biblical manuscripts and teaching myself ancient Greek eventually led me to P52, one of the earliest surviving fragments of the New Testament, and to recreating it by hand. Every facsimile is handcrafted by me personally, so no two are exactly alike. My hope is that each one gives you something tangible to see, hold, and study—and serves as a daily reminder of the Truth faithfully preserved for nearly two thousand years.
