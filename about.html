A Witness to the Truth

My fascination with biblical manuscripts isn't simply about history—it's about the faithfulness of God.

I created Schubert Scriptorium to bridge the gap between seeing a photograph of an ancient manuscript in a textbook and experiencing the physical reality of history. A manuscript fragment is more than ink on an old piece of material. It is a tangible connection to the people, places, and faith that carried the words of Scripture across the centuries.

My hope is that these facsimiles allow you to hold a small, powerful echo of the past—and to remember the countless faithful believers who carefully handwrote and handed-down God's Word to posterity, one generation at a time. And yet, through P52, we can see that God's Word remains reliable and trustworthy, matching what our Bible still say today.

Why P52 Matters

Dated to the early second century and written in ancient uncial Greek, Papyrus 52 (P52) is the oldest-known surviving manuscript fragment containing any portion of the New Testament. Though only a few inches in size, this tiny fragment gives us a remarkable glimpse into the early transmission of John's Gospel.

And what it preserves is extraordinary. P52 contains portions of John 18—the account of Jesus' trial before Pontius Pilate, just hours before His crucifixion.

"For this I was born and for this I have come into the world—to bear witness to the truth."

Immediately afterward comes Pilate's famous question:

"What is truth?"
