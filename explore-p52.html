01 • THE TEXT

The Greek Text

P52 preserves part of the Greek text of the Gospel of John. The surviving letters allow us to see an ancient physical witness to words that remain familiar to readers of the New Testament today.

Greek text can be placed here.

In a future version, individual Greek words can be highlighted and connected to their transliteration and English translation.

02 • JOHN 18

“What Is Truth?”

P52 contains part of the scene in which Jesus stands before Pontius Pilate. The surviving text includes Jesus' statement concerning His coming into the world to bear witness to the truth.

This makes P52 particularly meaningful to Schubert Scriptorium's theme: A Witness to the Truth.

The exact surviving Greek lines and corresponding English translation can be added here.

03 • HANDWRITING

Look Closely

Manuscripts are more than containers for words. The handwriting, spacing, abbreviations, margins, and arrangement of the text provide evidence about how ancient scribes produced and used Christian books.

Zoom in and examine the individual characters for yourself.

04 • THE ARTIFACT

Made of Papyrus

P52 is a physical artifact made from papyrus, an ancient writing material produced from the papyrus plant.

Its fibers, surface, folds, holes, and surviving edges are all part of the history of the object itself.

High-resolution photography makes details visible that can disappear in an ordinary photograph.

05 • FRAGMENT

What Is Missing?

P52 survives only as a fragment. Ancient manuscripts were physical objects that could be damaged, discarded, reused, or lost over the centuries.

The missing portions are therefore part of the story of the artifact.

Scholars distinguish between what is actually preserved and what can be reconstructed or inferred from other evidence.

06 • SIGNIFICANCE

A Witness to the Truth

P52 gives us a tangible connection to the transmission of the New Testament text.

It is a physical manuscript artifact that allows modern readers to examine an actual surviving witness rather than simply reading about one in a textbook.

Schubert Scriptorium exists in part to make that history tangible — to help people see that the text of Scripture has been transmitted through real manuscripts, real scribes, and real communities across generations.

Why P52 Matters

Papyrus 52 is small, but its story is enormous. It provides a physical window into the early transmission of the Gospel of John and gives visitors an opportunity to examine an ancient New Testament witness with their own eyes.
